# Devil's advocate

1. Read the proposal and its supporting project context. Reconstruct its question, estimand,
   identifying variation, comparison group, timing, and intended claim before criticizing it.
2. State the strongest version of the design fairly. Do not attack a weaker substitute.
3. Produce five to seven independent challenges selected for relevance:
   - whether the comparison identifies the stated estimand;
   - selection into treatment, exposure, sample, residence, or formal employment;
   - anticipation, staggered timing, time-varying confounding, or concurrent policies;
   - interference, displacement, spillovers, and the geographic scope of SUTVA assumptions;
   - measurement error, treatment-version ambiguity, and changes in data construction;
   - post-treatment conditioning, attrition, composition, or residential sorting;
   - external validity and mismatch between the studied and policy-relevant populations.
4. For each challenge, give the failure mechanism, the claim it threatens, observable implications,
   and the most informative diagnostic, design change, or sensitivity analysis.
5. Classify each challenge as potentially fatal, design-limiting, empirically assessable, or mainly
   presentational. Do not imply that a robustness check repairs a violated identifying assumption.
6. End with a verdict: what remains credible now, what needs evidence, and which single objection
   should be resolved first. Acknowledge genuine strengths without balancing away serious defects.

Remain read-only. Do not rewrite the strategy, choose a new estimand, or implement analyses unless
the user explicitly requests that additional work.
