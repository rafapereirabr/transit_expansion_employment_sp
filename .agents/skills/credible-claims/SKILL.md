Use before presenting a research brief, interpreting descriptive or model results, comparing proposed designs, or deciding whether language such as effect, impact, mechanism, or policy implication is warranted.

# Credible claims

1. Identify the exact claim, audience, product, population, period, outcome, exposure, and comparison.
   Preserve the project's separation between descriptive executive-box evidence, CAF policy
   analysis, and longer-term causal research.
2. Build the claim chain:
   `source data -> constructed measure -> empirical contrast -> identifying assumptions -> claim`.
   Mark every unsupported or unverified link.
3. Classify the strongest currently defensible statement as:
   - measurement or descriptive;
   - associational;
   - design-based but conditional on named assumptions;
   - causal for a defined estimand and population;
   - mechanism or policy extrapolation beyond the estimated contrast.
4. Check alignment among the estimand, unit, timing, exposure definition, comparison group, standard
   errors or uncertainty, and the population named in the prose. Treat null evidence, imprecision,
   and absence of a detected effect as distinct.
5. Identify the decisive assumptions and threats, including selection, anticipation, interference,
   spillovers, sorting, measurement, attrition, multiple testing, and external validity as relevant.
   A buffer, fixed effect, robustness table, or conventional estimator does not establish an
   assumption by itself.
6. Rewrite overstrong language to the strongest precise alternative. State the condition under
   which a stronger claim would become credible and the evidence most capable of changing the
   assessment.
7. Verify citations, quantities, dataset coverage, and method claims from primary sources when they
   matter. Do not expose restricted microdata or fine-geography outputs during verification.

Return: proposed claim, credibility class, supporting evidence, necessary assumptions, principal
threats, calibrated wording, and the next evidence needed.
