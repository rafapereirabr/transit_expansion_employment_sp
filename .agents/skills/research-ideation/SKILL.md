# Research ideation

1. Read `AGENTS.md`, the relevant sections of `MEMORY.md` and `BACKLOG.md`, and any supplied
   research notes. Keep the executive box, CAF report, and longer-term research design separate.
2. State the substantive question before naming a method. For each candidate question, distinguish
   the outcome, treatment or exposure, population, time horizon, and causal contrast.
3. Generate a small set of genuinely different candidates, including descriptive or mechanism
   questions when the available variation cannot support a causal claim.
4. For each candidate, specify:
   - the estimand and unit of analysis;
   - the source of identifying variation and proposed comparison;
   - the timing of treatment, outcome, and possible anticipation;
   - the assumptions required to interpret the comparison;
   - the main threats, including selection, sorting, interference, spillovers, and measurement;
   - the data structure and variation needed, without inventing fields or coverage;
   - evidence that would discriminate between competing explanations.
5. Compare candidates by substantive value, identification credibility, data feasibility, deadline,
   and sensitivity to assumptions. Do not rank novelty or feasibility from unverified literature or
   dataset claims.
6. Recommend the next informative decision or diagnostic, not an estimator by default. Mark
   unresolved choices for `interview-me` rather than silently fixing the estimand.
7. If the user requests a durable artifact, save it under `quality_reports/research_ideation/` with
   assumptions, open questions, and source links. Do not upload or reproduce restricted data.

Return a compact comparison followed by the strongest candidate and the reasons it currently leads.
Label descriptive, associational, and causal questions explicitly.
