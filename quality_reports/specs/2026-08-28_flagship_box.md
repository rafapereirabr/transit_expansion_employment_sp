# Requirements specification: CAF–BID flagship box on São Paulo and Santiago

**Date:** 2026-08-28  
**Status:** DRAFT — source reconciliation complete; three decisions remain open

## Objective

Prepare a brief box for the CAF–BID flagship chapter on transport and access to employment that
uses the São Paulo and Santiago studies to illustrate how improvements in urban accessibility can
translate into labor-market outcomes.

## Source hierarchy used

1. The 19 May 2026 email from Lucía Taboada is the direct instruction for this box.
2. The transport-and-employment chapter defines the surrounding argument and what would be
   duplicative.
3. The education chapter provides examples of possible box styles, not binding specifications.
4. The earlier 2–3 page exchange concerns Deliverable 1 / the methodological workplan, not this
   flagship box.

## Requirements

### Must

- [ ] Fit within one page for the box as a whole, unless CAF confirms that the limit applies to each
  city separately.
- [ ] Cover both São Paulo and Santiago, with findings identifiable by case.
- [ ] Connect accessibility improvements to labor outcomes, explicitly including the relevant
  subset of employment, income/wages, and formalization.
- [ ] Include brief motivation/context, a general description of the methodology, principal
  findings from each case, and policy implications.
- [ ] Use claims whose evidentiary status is explicit: descriptive, correlational, or causal.
- [ ] Avoid presenting preliminary accessibility comparisons as causal labor-market impacts.
- [ ] Complement rather than reproduce the flagship chapter's existing cross-city accessibility
  analysis.

### Should

- [ ] Include one compact visual if it communicates the main comparative result more efficiently
  than text.
- [ ] Use parallel mini-structures for São Paulo and Santiago so the cases can be compared quickly.
- [ ] Focus on vulnerable or lower-income populations when supported by each study.
- [ ] State the geographic coverage and limitations, especially because the flagship chapter's
  existing São Paulo analysis covers only the municipality and omits much of the metropolitan area.
- [ ] End with one or two policy implications tied directly to the evidence rather than generic
  advocacy for transit expansion.

### May

- [ ] Use a small comparison table instead of a conventional chart.
- [ ] Include a short “work in progress” qualifier if CAF accepts preliminary evidence.
- [ ] Mention accessibility as a mechanism or intermediate outcome alongside labor outcomes.

## Interpretation of the examples

- The examples vary substantially: one Santiago education box is only a study reference, two
  headline statistics, and a figure; other boxes are several explanatory paragraphs.
- This variation means the examples demonstrate acceptable rhetorical forms, not a fixed template
  or mandatory level of detail.
- Because the direct email gives a length and four content elements, it should govern whenever an
  example appears inconsistent.

## Success criteria

- [ ] A reader can identify the intervention, method, headline labor result, and implication for
  each city within one minute.
- [ ] The complete laid-out box fits on one flagship page at the publisher's actual dimensions.
- [ ] Every numerical claim has a source and an accurate evidence label.
- [ ] No content duplicates the surrounding chapter without adding a case-study result or mechanism.
- [ ] CAF confirms the length interpretation, acceptable evidence maturity, and current deadline.
